(01)Pipeline06 steps

Clip in. Short out.

Every Short goes through the same six steps, on your computer, in about the time it takes to watch it.

01

Find the heat

Pick the Twitch categories you care about and choose how it hunts: trending now ranks the last few hours by how fast clips are picking up views, top of the last 24 hours takes the biggest. Rankings are re-checked before every render, so a clip that went cold gets dropped.

02

Never twice

Every clip is remembered. The same moment clipped by someone else, or too close to something you posted last week, is skipped. The log tells you why: 88 filtered out (62 under 50 views…).

03

Hear every word

Speech-to-text runs on your own PC with faster-whisper, word by word. It uses your NVIDIA GPU when it can and quietly falls back to the CPU when it can’t. No audio leaves your machine.

04

Captions that pop

Big bold captions with the current word lit up, kept out of the Shorts buttons. Your font, colours and position. Reframed to 9:16 three ways: blurred fill, facecam over gameplay, or a straight crop.

05

A hook up front

Dead air is trimmed and the best moment flashes forward as a teaser, with two seconds of hook text on top, so the first frame is never someone loading in.

06

Titled, credited, posted

Two or three title styles to pick from, a description that always credits the streamer and links the clip, a thumbnail, then straight to YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. No accounts to connect? Download the MP4 and post it yourself.

(02)Two modesPer game, if you like

You approve, or it flies.

Start in review. When you trust what it picks, flip a game to autopilot and let the schedule do the rest.

Review queue

Nothing goes up until you say so.

  • Keeps rendering in the background until 20 Shorts are waiting (you set the number)
  • A 9:16 preview of each, with every title style side by side
  • Edit anything, approve, or skip. The next one is already rendered
Autopilot

Picks, renders and posts on a schedule.

  • Uploads every 2 hours by default, or any interval you like
  • A daily cap per game, so one category can’t take over your channel
  • Learns which title style gets your channel the most views and leans into it
(03)Under the hoodNo clipped server

Your keys. Your machine.

clipped is an app, not a service. It talks to Twitch and YouTube with your own free developer keys, and there is no account to make with me.

01

A real window

A frameless native window with its own title bar, a tray icon, and a sticker rail down the side. Close it while the engine runs and it keeps working from the tray.

02

Python + ffmpeg inside

The engine, faster-whisper and an ffmpeg build with libass for the captions all ship in the installer (the speech model downloads on first use). No Python to install, no console windows popping up.

03

AI titles, optional

Free templates by default. Paste a free Gemini key, sign in with Anthropic for Claude, connect OpenRouter with one click, or run a local Ollama model for free.

✓

Keys never leave home

Your Twitch, YouTube and AI keys are stored in the Windows Credential Manager and only ever sent to the service they belong to. Every description credits the streamer and links the original clip, and a model can’t remove that. Whether reposting a clip is OK is up to the streamer and the platforms, so review mode is the default.
(04)SetupAbout 15 minutes, once

Three steps, done once.

The app walks you through each one with a guide built in. You only do this once.

Twitch

Register a free app at dev.twitch.tv and paste the Client ID and Secret into Settings → Connections. About 3 minutes.

YouTube · Instagram

Connect YouTube (your own free Google Cloud project, about 10 minutes) and/or paste an Instagram token. Or skip both and download each Short to post yourself.

Go

Add the games you want, press the big button, and approve your first Shorts from the review queue.
